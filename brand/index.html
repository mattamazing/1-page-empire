Brand guidelines

Big headlines. Quiet everything else.

Persimmon and white, black type, and a lot of room to breathe. Built to read easily on a phone and to stand out in a feed.

Principles.

When a decision isn’t covered below, these settle it.

Readability comes first

Black text on white. Body text never below 18px. Reading lines stay under about 70 characters, even on desktop.

Loud headlines, quiet page

Headlines carry the weight. Everything around them stays plain: thin rules instead of boxes, no shadows, no gradients, no icons for decoration.

One orange, used boldly

Persimmon is the only brand color. Use it as big, confident fields (thumbnails, title cards, buttons) and as the logo, never as thin decoration. Headline text on white stays black.

Space is the luxury

If a section feels crowded, add space before adding anything else. Generous margins are what make a long page feel expensive.

Color.

Persimmon and white are the brand. Black is ink: body text and fine detail, not a background. Across everything, aim for mostly white, a strong dose of Persimmon, and black as text. Big bold headlines, button labels (19px bold or larger) and the logo can be white on Persimmon; body text on Persimmon is black. Dark backgrounds are only for stage slides, video end cards and dark-mode thumbnails, and use Charcoal (#1C1916), not pure black.

Don’t

  • Set the logo in black, gray, or any orange other than Persimmon. White is only for use on Persimmon.
  • Put a black logo on Persimmon. The edges shimmer and look blurry.
  • Use the wordmark below 20px tall. Switch to the A mark.
  • Place the logo on photos without a solid white or Persimmon field behind it.
  • Stretch, outline, add shadows to, or redraw the wordmark.

Matt Clark, personal brand

Matt’s personal brand uses this exact system: same orange, same type, same rules. Nothing on this page needs a separate version. The person and the company should feel like one thing.

Name wordmark

It’s just the headline type

Instrument Sans Bold at 85% width with −0.02em tracking, the same setting as every headline. No custom lettering, no icon, no monogram. It should read as confident plain text.

The name is never altered

No added punctuation, symbols, colors, or marks. The connection to Amazing comes from the shared type, the Persimmon backgrounds and the surroundings, not from changing the name.

Profile photo

On personal channels Matt’s face is the logo: a tight, well-lit headshot on a plain Linen (#F7F5F1) background with a Persimmon ring. Never a colored background behind the face: no teal, no Persimmon. Company channels use the A mark.

Using both together

  • One logo per graphic. Matt’s content leads with his name or face; Amazing’s offers lead with the Amazing logo.
  • When Matt’s content needs to point to the company, add a text line in Stone: “Founder of Amazing.” Not the Amazing logo.
  • On Amazing pages, Matt appears as a person (photo, name in normal headline type), not as a second logo.
  • If the name and the Amazing logo share a page, keep them in different places and at clearly different sizes, never side by side as equals.
  • Use the same handle everywhere where possible, and the same headshot across every personal channel at once.

Sentence case, everywhere

“Build an ecommerce brand you own,” not “Build An Ecommerce Brand You Own” or “BUILD AN ECOMMERCE BRAND.” This applies across all branding: web headlines, buttons, thumbnails, slides, social graphics, email subject lines and video titles. Title Case reads as an ad; sentence case reads as confident. The only all-caps word is the AMAZING logo itself.

Keep the whole headline one style

Drop the italic second phrase used on the current site. If a headline needs a second idea, put it in the lead paragraph below.

Long headlines are fine

The narrow width is built for 12 to 16 word direct-response headlines. Keep them big rather than shrinking them to fit.

Accent period on short headlines.

Short statement headlines of about four words or fewer end with a Persimmon period, like the section titles on this page. It puts a small dose of the brand color on every page while the headline stays black. Use .amz-dot.

Where the accent period doesn’t go

Not on long sentence headlines, questions, buttons, the logo, Matt’s name, thumbnails, or body text. On a Persimmon background, leave it off; on Charcoal it stays Persimmon. One per headline, only at the end.

Never bold whole paragraphs

Bold one short phrase per paragraph at most. If everything is emphasized, nothing is.

Spacing and layout.

Everything snaps to this scale. When in doubt, take the next size up.

Reading column: 640px

All sales copy sits in one centered column, .amz-measure. On a phone that is full width with 20px side margins.

Wide column: 1152px

Only for grids such as testimonials and plan comparisons, .amz-wide. They stack into one column under 768px.

Sections: 64px and 128px

Top and bottom padding is 64px on mobile and 128px on desktop, .amz-section. Separate sections with space or a Linen band, not boxes.

Real people, daylight, natural materials. Keep backgrounds neutral so the orange in titles and graphics is the only orange in the frame.

Real materialsLinen, oak, stone, paper, ceramic. Backgrounds that feel lived in, not staged offices.

Do and don’t.

The quickest checks before a page goes live.

Do

  • Use one primary button style per page and repeat it at every CTA.
  • Make buttons full width on mobile and at least 56px tall.
  • Let testimonials stand alone with space around them.
  • Use Persimmon as a big, bold field: thumbnails, title cards, banners, with white or black headlines.
  • Use the Linen band at most two or three times on a long page.
  • Test every page on a phone before anything else.

Don’t

  • Use Charcoal or black as the main background, or put small white text on Persimmon.
  • Use drop shadows, gradients, or rounded cards.
  • Color headlines or individual words orange.
  • Use gray body text. Gray is only for supporting text.
  • Center long paragraphs. Only short headlines and CTAs may be centered.

AMAZING BRAND BRIEF. Follow exactly. If a rule conflicts with a request, follow the rule and say so.

COLORS (use only these)
- Persimmon #E0582B: the one brand color. Logo, buttons, thumbnails, big color fields.
- Persimmon pressed #C94B22: button hover/active only.
- Persimmon tint #FCE6DC: light wash for callout boxes and soft sections.
- Persimmon text #A8401F: the only orange allowed for small text on white (kickers, labels, links).
- Black #000000: all body text and headlines on white.
- White #FFFFFF: page background; text on Persimmon when large and bold.
- Charcoal #1C1916: the only dark background. Never pure black backgrounds.
- Stone #6B665E: captions, fine print. Fog #E6E3DD: hairline borders. Linen #F7F5F1: occasional page band.
- Never use any other orange (no pumpkin, no coral, no gradients of orange).

BALANCE
- Mostly white, a strong dose of Persimmon, black as text.
- Black is ink, not a background. Dark (Charcoal) backgrounds only for: stage slides, video end cards, dark-mode thumbnails.
- Avoid compositions that are only orange + black: they read as Halloween. Add white.

TEXT ON PERSIMMON
- White: only for big bold text. Headlines, thumbnail words, button labels at 19px bold or larger, the logo. (3.8:1 contrast, passes for large text only.)
- Black: for everything smaller or longer (body copy, small labels, fine print). (5.6:1)
- Never small white text on Persimmon.

TEXT ON WHITE
- Body and headlines: black. Never gray body text; Stone is only for supporting text.
- Orange on white: Persimmon only for the logo and large display moments; small orange text uses #A8401F.
- Never color individual words or whole headlines orange.

LOGO
- Wordmark "AMAZING" and the A mark. Never redraw, stretch, outline, shadow, or retype it.
- Default: Persimmon logo on white. Bold version: white logo on Persimmon. Dark screens only: Persimmon logo on Charcoal.
- Never: black logo on Persimmon (edges shimmer), logo in gray or any other color, logo on a busy photo without a solid white or Persimmon field.
- Minimum wordmark height 20px on screen. Smaller than that, use the A mark.
- Avatars, app icon, favicon: white A mark (or white wordmark for round avatars) on a Persimmon square or circle.

MATT CLARK PERSONAL BRAND (same system as Amazing; no separate palette or fonts)
- Name wordmark: "Matt Clark" in Instrument Sans Bold, 85% width, -0.02em tracking. Plain text. Never alter the name: no added punctuation, symbols, colors, custom lettering, icon or monogram.
- Black name on white (default). White name on Persimmon. White name on Charcoal for dark screens only.
- Personal channels (YouTube, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, podcast): profile image is Matt's headshot on a plain Linen #F7F5F1 background with a Persimmon ring. No colored backgrounds behind the face (no teal, no Persimmon). Company channels use the A mark.
- One logo per graphic. Matt's content leads with his name or face; Amazing's offers lead with the Amazing logo.
- To connect Matt's content to the company, add the text line "Founder of Amazing" in Stone. Never place the name wordmark and the Amazing logo side by side at the same size.
- On Amazing pages, Matt appears as a person (photo, name in headline type), not as a second logo.

TYPOGRAPHY
- One family: Instrument Sans (Google Fonts). Headlines bold at 85-87.5% width, tight letter spacing (-0.02em). Body regular.
- Sentence case everywhere: web headlines, buttons, thumbnail words, slides, social graphics, email subject lines, video titles. Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns. No Title Case, no ALL CAPS (the AMAZING logo is the only exception).
- Body text at least 18px on web. Reading width about 640px.
- Bold at most one short phrase per paragraph.
- Accent period: short statement headlines (about four words or fewer) end with a period in Persimmon #E0582B, headline itself black. Examples: "Our programs." "Why Amazing." Not on long headlines, questions, buttons, the logo, Matt's name, thumbnails, or body text. Leave it off on Persimmon backgrounds; keep it Persimmon on Charcoal. One per headline, at the end only.

BUTTONS
- Persimmon background, white label, 19px bold, at least 56px tall, square-ish corners (2px radius).
- Full width on mobile. Hover: #C94B22.
- One primary button style per page, repeated at every call to action. Secondary: black outline on white.

YOUTUBE THUMBNAILS
- Default: Persimmon background, big white words, Matt's photo with a white ring.
- Keep the words heavy and to three or four words. If a title needs more words or thinner type, switch the text to black on Persimmon.
- Dark-mode variant: Charcoal background with Persimmon words. Use as a tactic, not the default.
- Neutral photo backgrounds. The only orange in the frame should be the brand graphics.

SLIDES
- White background, black headlines, one short Persimmon bar or the logo as the accent.
- Title or section cards may be full Persimmon with big white type.
- On stage, Charcoal slides are allowed: white headlines, Persimmon accents.

SOCIAL GRAPHICS
- Full Persimmon field with big white headline, small label in black. Or white field with black headline and a Persimmon element.

WEB PAGES
- White pages, black type, generous space. Sections separated by space or a Linen band, not boxes.
- No drop shadows, gradients, rounded cards, or decorative icons.
- Persimmon tint for one or two callout boxes per page (guarantee, key note).
- Test every page on a phone first.

PHOTOGRAPHY
- Real people, daylight, natural materials. Neutral backgrounds. No staged offices, no dramatic studio lighting.

Why we updated the brand

    
November 28, 2026

    

      
A brand doesn’t stand out because it picked the perfect color. It stands out because it’s distinctive and because it shows up the same way everywhere. Two principles drive every decision in this guide, drawn from Byron Sharp’s How Brands Grow and years of thinking about what makes brands memorable.

      
Where we’ve been. Amazing started with orange about ten years ago, and that’s the color people knew us by. A few years ago, as AI took off, we updated the logo and favicon with an “AI” play on Amazing Intelligence. More recently we used sea glass green for a short time to launch Amazing Alpha. AI is central to everything we do, but each change diluted what people recognized. This update returns to our orange, modernized, and commits to it.

      
1. Be distinctive: own something nobody else does. In our market, most business educators look alike: black, navy, gray. Nobody owns orange. We chose Persimmon because it’s open and it can be ours, not because it’s objectively the best orange. A color only becomes a brand asset when people link it to you and no one else.

      
2. Be prevalent: use it consistently, everywhere. Recognition comes from repetition. Every off-brand graphic, one-off color or “just this once” exception weakens the link we’re trying to build. That’s why we’re strict about consistency, even when it feels fussy: one orange, one logo, one typeface, used the same way on every page, slide, thumbnail and video. It’s also why Matt wears the same orange polo on camera. He’s the most-seen part of the brand, so he should carry the color too.

      
Consistency is the strategy, not a preference. If something here feels rigid, that’s the point.

      
What changed. We moved from sea glass green back to orange. Persimmon (#E0582B) stays close to the original Amazing orange and works as the logo color on white. Persimmon and white are now the core of the brand, black is used for text, and Charcoal is reserved for dark screens. Buttons and thumbnails use white type on Persimmon. The logo now has clear rules, including a white-on-Persimmon version and a favicon. Matt’s personal brand shares the same system instead of running separately, and the guide includes a brief that AI tools and designers can follow directly.
